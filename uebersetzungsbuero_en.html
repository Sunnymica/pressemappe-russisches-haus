Translation Office and the Russian House: Contradictory Address Statements

A commercial translation office listed the address of the Russian House as its business address until at least March 2026 – without indicating which building it is. Its own statements now contradict each other: the contact page and imprint name Georgenstraße 35, while the body text of the same homepage still refers to an office on Friedrichstraße (as of 14 July 2026). Whether premises on Friedrichstraße are still in use is unconfirmed.

🔍
Documented finding – as of 14 July 2026 Berlin Translation Center (berlintc.de) listed Friedrichstraße 176–179 – the address of the Russian House – as its business address until at least March 2026. Since July 2026 at the latest, its footer and contact page name Georgenstraße 35 (6th floor, room 609), while the body text of the same homepage still refers to an office on Friedrichstraße. Should rental payments for premises in the Russian House benefit the listed organisation, this would, according to legal experts cited by the NZZ, be relevant under EU sanctions law.

📋 What we know – verified facts +
  • Berlin Translation Center (berlintc.de) listed Friedrichstraße 176–179, 10117 Berlin-Mitte – the address of the Russian House – as its business address until at least March 2026; since July 2026 at the latest, footer and contact page name Georgenstraße 35, while the homepage body text still mentions an office on Friedrichstraße (as of 14 July 2026)berlintc.de, retrieved March 2026
  • The office offers certified translations in Russian, Ukrainian and German – as well as express translations in 30 minutes during business hours
  • The website is available in four language versions: German, English, Russian and Ukrainian
  • According to a report by the NZZ (November 2025), a translation office was registered on the third floor of Friedrichstraße 176–179NZZ, 1 November 2025
  • The office ranked prominently in search results for "certified translation Ukrainian Berlin" and similar queries, listing the Friedrichstraße address – nothing on the website indicated that this is the building whose operator is EU-sanctioned
  • Any rental income from subletting would – if it benefits the listed organisation – violate the EU sanctions against Rossotrudnichestvo, according to legal experts cited by the NZZ; the actual payment route is not publicly knownNZZ, November 2025
🎯 Why Ukrainians could end up there +

Berlin has a number of reputable, independent translation services for Ukrainian and Russian – from individual sworn translators to larger agencies. No documented shortage of these services exists.

Berlin Translation Center advertises a practical advantage: express service in 30 minutes, in person, in a central location. Anyone who urgently needed a certified translation for an appointment could search online, find the office near the top of the results – and go there. The address "Friedrichstraße 176–179" raised no suspicion.

Most recently, the website still referred to an office on Friedrichstraße – that this meant, or means, premises in the Russian House, whose operator Rossotrudnichestvo is EU-sanctioned, is not apparent from it. Anyone unfamiliar with the connection had no reason to investigate further.

⚠️ Why this matters under sanctions law +
  • Rossotrudnichestvo has been on the EU sanctions list since July 2022: all assets frozen, provision of funds or economic resources prohibited
  • Subletting space in the Russian House generates income. Should this income benefit Rossotrudnichestvo, it could constitute a commercial activity benefiting a sanctioned entity
  • The NZZ cites legal experts who consider this a potential sanctions violation – Berlin prosecutors are already investigating similar arrangements involving tenants in the building
  • Whether the translation office is part of the ongoing investigation is not known
🔍 What remains unclear +
  • If Berlin Translation Center rented or sublet premises at Friedrichstraße 176–179: who was the contractual counterparty – and for what period?
  • Does rental income flow to Rossotrudnichestvo or to another entity within the building?
  • Are customs authorities and prosecutors aware of the translation office as a subtenant?
  • Have any Ukrainian clients ever been explicitly directed to this address by German authorities?
Note on context Berlin Translation Center is a legitimately registered company with a sworn translator. There is no evidence that it knowingly acts in the interests of the Russian House or Russia. The findings concern the location and the potential rental income – not the quality of the translation services provided.

Open questions for politicians and authorities

  • Did a tenancy or subletting arrangement for premises in the Russian House exist, or does it still – and are Berlin prosecutors aware of it?
  • Does rental income flow to Rossotrudnichestvo – and would this violate EU sanctions?
  • Have any Berlin authorities or job centres ever directed Ukrainians to the Berlin Translation Center address?
  • Why does the office appear in search results without any indication that it is located inside the sanctioned Russian House?
