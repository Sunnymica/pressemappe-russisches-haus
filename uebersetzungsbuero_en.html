Translation Bureau and Russian House: Conflicting Address Details

Until at least March 2026, a commercial translation agency listed the address of the Russian House as its business address without disclosing the building’s context. Today, the provider’s own representations conflict. While the contact page and legal notice point to Georgenstraße 35, the body text on the exact same homepage continues to reference an office on Friedrichstraße. Whether active operations are still being conducted at the Friedrichstraße location remains unconfirmed.

🔍
Verified Findings – As of July 14, 2026 Until at least March 2026, the Berlin Translation Center (berlintc.de) listed Friedrichstraße 176–179 as its business address, which corresponds directly to the Russian House. Since July 2026 at the latest, the website's footer and contact pages have cited Georgenstraße 35 (6th floor, room 609), whereas the homepage’s body text continues to mention an office on Friedrichstraße. Legal experts interviewed by the NZZ point out that if rent payments for premises in the Russian House directly or indirectly benefit the sanctioned operating entity, this would carry significant sanctions implications.

📋 Verified Facts +
  • The Berlin Translation Center (berlintc.de) listed its business address as Friedrichstraße 176–179, 10117 Berlin-Mitte until at least March 2026, which is the physical address of the Russian House.berlintc.de, accessed March 2026 (archived evidence being processed)
  • Since July 2026 at the latest, the footer and contact pages on berlintc.de have specified Georgenstraße 35 (6th floor, room 609) as the office location, though the body text on the same homepage continues to reference an office on Friedrichstraße. No confirmation has been provided as to whether these premises remain actively in use.berlintc.de, accessed July 14, 2026
  • The agency offers certified translation services in Russian, Ukrainian, and German, promoting express turnaround times of within 30 minutes during regular working hours.
  • The provider's website is published in four language editions: German, English, Russian, and Ukrainian.
  • According to a report published by the Swiss daily NZZ on November 1, 2025, a translation agency was registered on the third floor of Friedrichstraße 176–179.NZZ, November 1, 2025
  • Online search queries such as “certified Ukrainian translation Berlin” returned the agency with the Friedrichstraße 176–179 address. Nothing on the agency’s website indicated that this location is inside the building of an EU-sanctioned entity.
  • Legal experts cited by the NZZ indicate that if rental revenues from sub-leasing transactions benefit the designated operating organization, this would constitute a potential violation of the EU sanctions imposed against Rossotrudnichestvo. The actual payment pathways are not publicly known.NZZ, November 2025
🎯 Why Ukrainian Customers May End Up There +

Berlin is home to numerous independent translation bureaus for Ukrainian and Russian, ranging from sworn freelance translators to full-service agencies. There is no documented general shortage of these services in the city.

However, the Berlin Translation Center markets highly practical advantages: 30-minute express translations, walk-in services, and a central location in Berlin-Mitte. Individuals who urgently require certified translations for official appointments are likely to find the bureau occupying top search engine results and head directly to the office. The plain street address “Friedrichstraße 176–179” offers no immediate cause for suspicion.

Until recently, the homepage explicitly directed clients to a Friedrichstraße office. The fact that these premises are located inside the Russian House, operated by the EU-sanctioned federal agency Rossotrudnichestvo, is not disclosed on the website. Without prior knowledge of this connection, customers have no apparent reason to investigate further.

⚠️ Relevance to Sanctions Regulations +
  • The operating entity, Rossotrudnichestvo, was added to the EU sanctions list in July 2022. The measures require the freezing of all assets and prohibit making any funds or economic resources available to the organization.OpenSanctions
  • Sub-leasing commercial space inside the Russian House generates revenue. If these rental payments flow to Rossotrudnichestvo, they may constitute commercial activity benefiting a sanctioned institution.
  • The NZZ references legal scholars who view such lease arrangements as a potential violation of sanctions laws. The Berlin Public Prosecutor's Office is already conducting investigations into similar sub-leasing transactions within the building.
  • Whether this specific translation bureau is a subject of the ongoing investigations remains unknown.
🔍 Unresolved Questions +
  • If the Berlin Translation Center rented or sub-leased space at Friedrichstraße 176–179, who was the direct contractual partner, and what was the exact duration of the lease agreement?
  • Do rental payments flow to Rossotrudnichestvo, or are they directed through an intermediary entity operating within the building?
  • Are the responsible German customs authorities or the Public Prosecutor’s Office aware of this sub-tenant’s presence?
  • Have German government offices or job centers ever explicitly directed Ukrainian clients to this specific address?

Note on Context and Classification The Berlin Translation Center is a properly registered business operated by a sworn translator. There is no evidence suggesting that the agency knowingly acts in the interest of the Russian House or the Russian Federation. These findings pertain solely to the physical location and its associated rental revenues, and do not reflect on the professional quality of the translation services provided.

Outstanding Questions for Policymakers and Authorities

  • Did or does a sub-lease agreement exist for space inside the Russian House, and are Berlin investigative authorities aware of it?
  • Do rental payments flow to Rossotrudnichestvo, and does this constitute a breach of active EU sanctions?
  • Have Berlin administrative bodies or job centers recommended the Berlin Translation Center’s address to Ukrainian refugees during consultations?
  • Why did the provider’s website never disclose that its business address is located within the building of the sanctioned Rossotrudnichestvo agency, and what is the reason for the current discrepancies in its address listings?
