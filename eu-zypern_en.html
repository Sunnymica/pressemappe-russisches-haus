Cyprus country file

Continued presence despite the EU listing

The centre in Nicosia is not a mere partner network but an official Rossotrudnichestvo representation. It existed long before 2022 and, after the fire of 2023, is documented holding events on its own premises in 2024 and again in 2026. No termination, suspension or cancellation by the Republic of Cyprus was found – but neither was any separate status agreement.

Three levels Cyprus keeps particularly clear

Cyprus is an example of continued presence despite the EU listing. The main lesson is methodological: EU listing, national enforcement and closure are three different levels. The Cypriot sanctions framework of 2025 is general in nature; no order specific to the centre concerning accounts, property or tax was found. Reading rule: “Open” means the statement is not documented in the public sources examined. It is not proof of the contrary. EU listing, national enforcement, political non-cooperation, treaty termination and actual closure of operations are assessed separately.

Legal basis

The Cypriot legal basis is general in nature: a cultural and scientific agreement of 1972 whose continuation a protocol of 2000 confirms pending new arrangements. The Cypriot ratification law 34(III)/2001 is published.

https://www.cylaw.org

Article 5 of the protocol requires the last written notification for its entry into force. That date was not found – and the status of the protocol in 2026 is therefore not conclusively verified either.

https://www.cylaw.org

A separate agreement on the status, privileges, tax or ownership questions of the centre could not be verified either. The general cultural agreement basis is therefore weaker and less detailed than an express centre arrangement – as in Spain or Slovenia.

https://www.cylaw.org

Measures since 2022

No termination, suspension or cancellation by the Republic of Cyprus was found. In March 2022 Cyprus commented in general terms on EU sanctions enforcement, without reference to the centre.

https://www.reuters.com

With laws 149(I)/2025 and 150(I)/2025 Cyprus created a tightened enforcement framework as from 25 July 2025, including a National Sanctions Implementation Unit. That framework is general; no order specific to the centre concerning accounts, property, assets or tax was found.

https://www.gov.cy

The fire of 26 April 2023 was not a state act. The course of attribution is notable: the Russian side initially spoke of a possible attack, while the Cypriot police, according to media accounts, found no evidence of arson.

https://www.reuters.com
https://cyprus-mail.com

Assessment

Continued presence – and the limits of transferability

Cyprus is an example of continued presence despite the EU listing. For Germany the main lesson is methodological: EU listing, national enforcement and closure are three different levels that must not be translated into one another.

The general cultural agreement basis is also weaker and less detailed than an express centre arrangement. Where no status treaty exists, the clauses on which a termination might fasten are missing too.

Without clarification of ownership, accreditation and accounts the Cypriot case is therefore only transferable to a limited extent. It serves as evidence that a presence can continue – not as evidence that it is unobjectionable under sanctions law.
