Country file

Denmark

Regular operations ended – later used intermittently, according to Russian sources

Regular in-person operations ended Embassy and partner network active Residual legal status unresolved

Copenhagen was home to an official Russian Centre for Science and Culture operated by Rossotrudnichestvo. Its regular operations ended in late September 2023 after Denmark imposed a staffing cap on the Russian mission as a whole. Denmark did not issue a separate closure order against the centre itself. Since 2025, the Russian embassy has reported individual events at a venue it continues to describe as the Russian House or RCSC. Those reports do not establish that the former full-scale operation of the centre has resumed.

Key finding: Denmark effectively shut down the centre through a diplomatic staffing measure, but neither terminated the underlying cultural agreement nor formally closed the institution. The later intermittent use of the venue illustrates the limits of that approach.

At a glance

Centre Russian Centre for Science and Culture, also known as the Russian House
Operator Rossotrudnichestvo until regular operations ceased; later use reported by the Russian embassy
Location Vester Voldgade 11, 1552 Copenhagen
Agreement Agreement on cooperation in culture, science and education, signed on 4 November 1993 and in force since 12 February 1994; terminable on six months’ notice
Opening Predecessor structure dating back to the 1970s; the building was acquired by the Soviet state in 1975 and the “Venskabshuset” opened in 1977
Measure Cap imposed in September 2023 limiting the Russian mission to five diplomats and 20 administrative and technical staff
Status Regular operations ended; absent from Denmark’s diplomatic list since 2024; intermittent event use reported by Russian sources since 2025
Rationale Russian attempts to secure visas for intelligence officers and Denmark’s decision to align Russian staffing levels with those of the Danish embassy in Moscow

Summary

As recently as early 2023, the Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs listed the Russian Centre for Science and Culture as a separate component of Russia’s official presence. The entry gave the address at Vester Voldgade 11, an official rs.gov.ru email address and a dedicated Rossotrudnichestvo website. Danish diplomatic list, January 2023

On 1 September 2023, Denmark capped the Russian mission at five diplomats and 20 administrative and technical staff. According to the centre’s own account, 26 September was its final regular working day, while 27 September was cited as the date on which it closed its doors. The measure applied to the Russian mission as a whole and was not a separate cultural-policy decision directed specifically at the centre.

The RCSC has not appeared in Danish diplomatic lists since January 2024. Russian embassy channels have, however, reported individual events since May 2025 at a venue they continue to call the Russian Centre for Science and Culture or the Russian House. Those reports show that the site has been used again for selected events, but they do not prove that an independent Rossotrudnichestvo mission with regular public operations has been restored.

Legal basis

The relevant instrument is the Agreement between the Kingdom of Denmark and the Russian Federation on cooperation in culture, science and education, signed on 4 November 1993. It entered into force on 12 February 1994 and replaced a Danish-Soviet cultural agreement dating from 1962. Treaty text in the United Nations Treaty Series

Article 21: cultural centres

Article 21 provides that both parties are to support the establishment of a Danish cultural centre in Russia and a Russian cultural centre in Denmark. Their legal status and operating conditions were to be settled in a separate arrangement. No published supplementary, status or implementing agreement has so far been located. That does not exclude the possibility that an administrative arrangement or exchange of notes once existed.

Article 26: termination

Denne overenskomst vil forblive gyldig indtil 6 måneder efter at en af parterne skriftligt giver den anden part meddelelse om sit ønske om at opsige den.

English translation: This Agreement shall remain in force until six months after one party has notified the other in writing of its wish to terminate it.

The agreement is therefore terminable on six months’ notice. There is no evidence that Denmark has invoked that clause. In 2023, Denmark chose a staffing measure rather than the treaty route.

Measures since 2022

Expulsion of Russian intelligence officers

In April 2022, Denmark expelled 15 Russian intelligence officers. The Danish Security and Intelligence Service (PET) later assessed that the expulsions had significantly reduced Russia’s ability to collect intelligence on Danish soil. PET annual report 2022

No official source establishes a direct link between those expelled and the cultural centre. General descriptions of Russian intelligence methods do not justify labelling the RCSC itself as a proven intelligence facility.

EU sanctions against Rossotrudnichestvo

On 21 July 2022, the European Union added Rossotrudnichestvo to its sanctions list. The designation also led to the freezing of funds and economic resources in Denmark, but it did not automatically close the centre. Official Journal of the European Union, 21 July 2022

Release of essential operating funds

In March 2023, the Danish authorities confirmed that approximately DKK 26,000 per month had been released from frozen Russian funds to cover essential expenses at the Russian House. This was not a Danish subsidy, but a use of Russian funds permitted under the EU sanctions regime. Reply from the Danish Ministry of Industry, Business and Financial Affairs

I den konkrete sag vedrørende Ruslands Hus […] udstedt en sådan tilladelse til frigivelse af ca. 26.000 kr. hver måned.

English translation: In the specific case concerning the Russian House, such an authorisation was issued for the release of approximately DKK 26,000 each month.

Staffing cap on the Russian mission

On 1 September 2023, the Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs informed the Russian ambassador that the size of the Russian mission would be brought into line with that of the Danish embassy in Moscow. The reduction had to be completed by 29 September. Statement by the Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs

Konkret betyder det et loft på 5 diplomater og 20 administrativt og teknisk personale.

English translation: In practical terms, this means a ceiling of five diplomats and 20 administrative and technical staff.

The measure targeted the Russian mission as a whole, not cultural activity or the RCSC specifically. The centre’s regular operation ended as a practical consequence of that staffing cap.

Government rationale in the original

The Danish Ministry of Foreign Affairs cited unsuccessful visa negotiations and repeated Russian attempts to include visas for intelligence officers in those talks.

Rusland har gentagne gange forsøgt at få visum til efterretningsofficerer som en del af forhandlingerne.

English translation: Russia has repeatedly attempted to make visas for intelligence officers part of the negotiations.

The official rationale was therefore diplomatic and security-related. Denmark did not separately declare that the cultural centre had to be closed because of propaganda or disinformation.

Russian accounts of later use

Since May 2025, the Russian embassy has reported several events at a venue it continues to describe as the Russian Centre for Science and Culture or the Russian House. The reports include a Victory Day anniversary event, an exhibition and other cultural gatherings. Russian embassy report dated 7 May 2025

These Russian government sources show that the site was once again used publicly for selected events and labelled accordingly. On their own, however, they do not establish regular opening hours, language courses, library services, dedicated Rossotrudnichestvo staff or renewed inclusion in Denmark’s diplomatic list.

The defensible status is therefore not “reopened”, but: intermittent event use reported by Russian sources; institutional residual status unresolved.

Parallel structures

Embassy school

A school attached to the Russian embassy continues to operate separately from the former RCSC. It is a Russian state educational institution and must not be conflated with the cultural centre.

Compatriots’ organisation and Russkiy Mir

Copenhagen is also home to a civic organisation using the name “Russian House”. In 2021, the Russkiy Mir Foundation reported the opening of a Russkiy Mir Cabinet within that organisation. It is neither a diplomatic mission nor automatically a Rossotrudnichestvo office. Russkiy Mir, 2021 report

Cooperation with and material support from Russkiy Mir demonstrate institutional links, but do not by themselves establish full state control over every activity.

No documented transfer to a neighbouring country

No formal transfer of Denmark-related Rossotrudnichestvo functions to Stockholm, Oslo, Helsinki or another regional office has been documented. Russian activity appears instead to have continued through the embassy, the embassy school, compatriots’ organisations, Russkiy Mir partners and occasional events at the former centre.

Property and legal status

Danish historical accounts state that the Soviet state acquired the building at Vester Voldgade 11 in 1975. From 1977, the Danish-Soviet friendship association used it as the “Venskabshuset”. The Russian state later took over operation of the cultural centre. Historical account by Kim Frederichsen

The current land-registry entry could not be verified through freely accessible official sources. Nor has any special diplomatic, consular or treaty-based immunity for the property been established. The centre’s former inclusion in the diplomatic list does not by itself prove that the premises were inviolable.

Assessment

Denmark represents a distinct category within the Europe dossier. The official centre was not terminated by treaty notice, as in the Republic of Moldova, nor closed by a measure directed specifically at the institution. Instead, a general diplomatic staffing cap removed the practical personnel base for regular operations.

That approach was initially effective: regular activities ceased and the RCSC disappeared from Denmark’s diplomatic list. Because the cultural agreement remained in force and the premises apparently remained available, the Russian side was later able to report individual events there again.

Lesson for Berlin: Staffing and financial restrictions can severely disrupt day-to-day operations. Lasting institutional clarity, however, requires the legal agreement, formal status, staffing structure and property position to be addressed as well.
