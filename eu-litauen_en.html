Lithuania country file

Never opened – and still not demolished

The key finding is this: Moscow House in Vilnius never entered regular operation. It must therefore be described neither as a “Russian House closed after February 2022” nor as a former Rossotrudnichestvo presence. Nor has the demolition been carried out: the building was still standing on 1 August 2026. Lithuania shows how planning and building law can work as an alternative to sanctions law – and how long enforcement takes.

At a glance

Project Moscow House Vilnius – a cultural and business facility sponsored by Moscow
Status Never regularly opened. No on-site and no digital offering
Security assessment The Lithuanian State Security Department VSD stated as early as 2017 that Russia had not managed to establish an institution of comparable status in Lithuania – such as a Rossotrudnichestvo centre
Building law The building permit was declared definitively invalid in November 2017
Court decision In 2021 the Supreme Administrative Court, referring to the VSD assessment, found the project incompatible with national security interests

Enforcement Not completed. Tender in 2024, challenge in 2026, insolvency of the developer; on 1 August 2026 signatures and judicial confirmation of a privately financed arrangement were still missing
To distinguish A historic Russkiy Mir partner centre at what was then the Lithuanian University of Educational Sciences is documented (2009 to 2017); no reliable continuation could be found

The precise status is: never opened

Two claims require correction here. First: the building was never opened – there can be no talk of a “closure”. Second: a completed demolition must not be asserted. On 1 August 2026 negotiations were still under way between the insolvency administration, the building inspectorate and the city; signatures and judicial confirmation were missing. A legally binding demolition order is not an actual demolition. Reading rule: “Open” means the statement is not documented in the public sources examined. It is not proof of the contrary. EU listing, national enforcement, political non-cooperation, treaty termination and actual closure of operations are assessed separately.

Legal basis

Lithuania relies neither on a cultural agreement nor primarily on sanctions law, but on planning and building law combined with a national security assessment. The building permit was declared definitively invalid in 2017.

https://vilnius.lt

In 2021 the Supreme Administrative Court, referring to the VSD assessment, found the project incompatible with national security interests. The court decision of 9 December 2022 opened the way for the state building inspectorate to demolish.

https://vilnius.lt
https://www.lrt.lt

The full text of the Regional Court decision of 8 July 2026, and the ownership, insolvency and land-register position, could not be fully evaluated.

https://www.vz.lt

Measures since 2022

The measures are directed at removing an unlawful structure, not at closing operations – there never were any. Tender, penalty payments and substitute performance are the instruments used.

https://am.lrv.lt

Enforcement has so far foundered on insolvency, ownership questions and challenges. The court decision of 8 July 2026 even halted the planned transfer to the city. On 1 August 2026 the building was still standing.

Assessment

Planning law as an alternative – with long enforcement

Lithuania illustrates an alternative to sanctions law: planning and building law combined with a national security assessment can permanently stop a project that never opened. What is transferable is the careful examination of existing permits and their actual use.

What is not transferable is the claim that a centre was “closed”. The precise status is: never opened. That distinction is not pedantry – it determines whether Lithuania can serve as a test case for closure elsewhere. It cannot.

The protracted enforcement also carries a general lesson: a legally binding demolition order is not yet an actual demolition. Years and several failed attempts can lie between a court decision and physical removal.
