Europe dossier · Croatia country file

The Russian House that never quite became one

For years, Russia pursued the opening of a formal cultural and scientific centre in Zagreb. Croatia even prepared a special agreement governing such centres — but never ratified it. What operated instead was an embassy-linked Rossotrudnichestvo representation that later adopted the public brand “Russian House Zagreb” and then disappeared from public view after 2022, without any identifiable Croatian closure order.

Status
Former representation no longer traceable No official Russian House established
Core finding

Romania closed a formally established centre. In Croatia, by contrast, a never fully institutionalised Rossotrudnichestvo representation disappeared from the publicly visible structure. The planned special agreement for a genuine cultural centre was never ratified.

What the official map shows

Croatia is absent from Rossotrudnichestvo’s current map and central location listing. A recent comparative study likewise states that Croatia has neither a Russian Centre nor a Russkiy Mir cabinet, and that the activities of the “Russian House” in Zagreb are suspended. Timeline

18 December 1998

General cultural cooperation agreement

Croatia and Russia sign an agreement in Moscow on cultural cooperation. It promotes cultural contacts, exchange programmes, translation, film, youth exchanges and the mutual circulation of cultural information. It neither establishes nor expressly mentions a Russian cultural centre in Zagreb. Source: Narodne Novine

The agreement entered into force on 12 May 1999. Source: entry into force

10 December 2004

Russia announces plans for a cultural centre

At a meeting between foreign ministers Miomir Žužul and Sergei Lavrov, Croatia declares negotiations over Russia’s clearing debt to have been completed. In the same official statement, Russia confirms its intention to open a Russian cultural centre in Zagreb and to build a new Russian Embassy. Source: Croatian Foreign Ministry

The two projects appear in the same political context but are presented separately. There is no evidence that the cultural centre formed part of the debt settlement or was offered in exchange for it.

2006–2011

Settlement of Soviet-era debt

The two governments regulate liabilities inherited from Soviet-Yugoslav trade, with Russia settling the debt through agreed goods and services. This period of economic normalisation forms the backdrop against which Moscow continued to pursue the cultural-centre project. No legal linkage between the two has been established. Source: bilateral treaties list

2013

Special agreement on information and cultural centres planned

The Croatian government places legislation ratifying an agreement with Russia on “the establishment and operating conditions of information and cultural centres” on its programme for the third quarter of 2013. Responsibility lies with the Ministry of Culture. Source: 2013 government programme

This means that a dedicated centres agreement was at least far advanced. Whether it had already been signed, merely initialled or existed as an agreed draft remains unclear.

2015

Ratification expressly recorded as “not implemented”

The Croatian Culture Ministry’s annual report again lists the planned ratification legislation. Its implementation status is recorded as “Nije provedeno” — “Not implemented”. Source: Culture Ministry

The crucial distinction Croatia planned a formal centre before Russia’s annexation of Crimea. After 2014, the ratification needed to create that centre appears not to have gone ahead.
May 2018

Rossotrudnichestvo representation documented

Despite the absence of a formal centre, a “Rossotrudnichestvo representation in Croatia” operates in Zagreb. It co-organises an international academic conference with the Croatian Institute of History. The programme expressly identifies Natalia Yakimchuk and Sergei Zhdanovich as members of the representation. Source: conference programme

2019

Public cultural activity without a documented centre building

The representation supports concerts and maintains its own stand at Zagreb’s Interliber book fair. Events take place at venues including the Academy of Music. No dedicated centre address is evident from these activities. Source: event report Source: Interliber floor plan

16 December 2020

Lavrov still describes the centre as a future project

During a visit to Zagreb, Foreign Minister Sergei Lavrov again states that Russia is interested in opening a cultural and scientific centre. This is a particularly important indication that the existing representation was not, in Russian eyes, equivalent to a fully established centre. Source: HRT

October 2021

The representation adopts the “Russian House Zagreb” brand

At the first Russian animation film festival in Zagreb and Pula, Rossotrudnichestvo is explicitly named as the initiator. The “Russian House Zagreb” simultaneously organises a children’s drawing competition. The branding was therefore real and public, but appears to have referred to the representation as a label rather than to an independently established centre created under the planned special agreement. Source: Nacional

10–11 March 2022

Security context: military drone crashes in Zagreb

A Soviet-designed military drone arriving from the Ukrainian war zone via Romania and Hungary crashes in Zagreb’s Jarun district near a student residence. The Croatian government is unable to establish publicly whether it had been operated by the Russian or Ukrainian side. The incident nevertheless makes clear that the war can reach Croatian territory. Source: Croatian government

No direct link to the later reduction of Russian Embassy staff has been documented.

23 March 2022

Manipulated IKEA images spread through the “Russian House” channel

The Facebook page “Ruski dom u Zagrebu” circulates images supposedly showing portraits of Vladimir Putin in picture frames at IKEA’s Zagreb store. IKEA tells the Croatian fact-checker Faktograf that the images were manipulated. Source: Faktograf

This documents a concrete shift: a Rossotrudnichestvo page presenting itself as a cultural representation spread a false pro-Russian story after the start of the full-scale invasion.

11 April 2022

Croatia sharply reduces the Russian Embassy

Croatia summons the Russian ambassador and orders 18 diplomats and six administrative and technical staff to leave the country. The measure affects 24 employees in total. The Russian Embassy is thereby reduced to the staffing level of the Croatian Embassy in Moscow. Source: Croatian Foreign Ministry

No public list of names exists. It is therefore impossible to establish whether members of the Rossotrudnichestvo representation were among those expelled. The measure combined a sharp political response to the war with the principle of reciprocity. The drastic reduction may explain the later loss of capacity of an embassy-linked representation, but that remains a plausible reconstruction rather than an officially confirmed causal link.

21 July 2022

EU sanctions Rossotrudnichestvo

The European Union places Rossotrudnichestvo on its sanctions list. The Council describes it as a central Russian state body for soft power and hybrid influence, funding propaganda projects, connecting pro-Russian actors and disseminating Kremlin narratives and historical revisionism. Source: EUR-Lex

July 2023

VIGINUM places the page within Russia’s information network

The French agency VIGINUM includes “Ruski dom u Zagrebu” in its investigation into the Russian information campaign RRN. The page is listed as part of a diplomatic communications network that amplified campaign content. Source: VIGINUM

Together with the IKEA case, this provides a second indication that the digital “House” structure was not limited to cultural programming but formed part of the Russian state information environment.

August 2025

Ukrainian intelligence service reports operations ended

Ukraine’s Foreign Intelligence Service states that Russian Houses ceased operating in several countries, including Croatia, after the start of the full-scale invasion. The source gives no date, legal act or responsible authority. Source: Ukrainian Foreign Intelligence Service

July 2026

No centre; “Russian House” activities described as suspended

A current comparative study states that Croatia has neither a Russian Centre nor a Russkiy Mir cabinet and that the activities of the “Russian House” in Zagreb are suspended. Croatia is also absent from Rossotrudnichestvo’s current map and public location presentation. Source: Konrad Adenauer Foundation Map and fact check

Legal basis

1. General cultural agreement of 1998

The agreement commits both states to cultural exchange but does not establish a specific cultural centre. Article 12 merely allows the competent institutions to conclude their own implementing arrangements. Source: agreement text

2. Duration and termination

Article 13 provides for five-year terms and automatic renewal for further five-year periods unless written notice is given at least six months before expiry. Source: Article 13

Because the agreement entered into force on 12 May 1999, the current term would appear, as a matter of calculation, to run from 12 May 2024 to 12 May 2029. That is an inference from the treaty text, not a published position of the Croatian government.

As in Germany and Poland, the agreement combines automatic renewal with a six-month notice period. Unlike those cases, however, this treaty did not itself establish a Russian House.

3. The special centres agreement that was never ratified

In 2013, Croatia expressly planned legislation to ratify a special agreement governing the establishment and operation of information and cultural centres. In 2015, the Culture Ministry recorded the measure as “not implemented”. Source: 2013 government programme Source: 2015 annual report

No such agreement appears in Croatia’s current official list of bilateral treaties with Russia. This strongly suggests that the later representation did not enjoy a special bilateral status as a cultural centre.

4. Probable status of the representation

On the evidence available, this was a Russian state representation that organised events at changing Croatian venues, operated under the Rossotrudnichestvo name and was digitally linked to Russia’s diplomatic communications network.

No separate legal entity, dedicated centre building or special diplomatic immunity has yet been established.

Measures since 2022

No formal Croatian decision stating that the “Russian House Zagreb is hereby closed” has been identified. What can be established is:

  • the substantial reduction of Russian Embassy staff in April 2022
  • the EU listing of Rossotrudnichestvo in July 2022
  • Croatia’s subsequent disappearance from Rossotrudnichestvo’s visible structure
  • later sources describing the activity as ended or suspended

Foreign Ministry: April 2022 EUR-Lex: July 2022 Ukrainian intelligence service KAS: July 2026

Most likely sequence — not officially confirmed Croatia did not close a stand-alone cultural centre through a specific legal act. Instead, the embassy-linked Rossotrudnichestvo representation appears to have lost its operational basis after the reduction of the embassy and the imposition of EU sanctions. Regular public activity is no longer traceable; later sources describe the operation as suspended. No Croatian document expressly confirming that causal sequence has been found.

Government rationale in the original

No specific official explanation for the suspension of the “Russian House” has been identified. The closest relevant government statement is the Croatian Foreign Ministry’s announcement of 11 April 2022 concerning the reduction of Russian Embassy staff.

Croatian original
„najoštrija osuda brutalne agresije na Ukrajinu i brojnih počinjenih zločina“
English translation
“the strongest condemnation of the brutal aggression against Ukraine and of the numerous crimes committed”

The ministry also demanded an immediate end to military activities, the withdrawal of Russian forces from the whole of Ukraine and accountability for the crimes committed. Source: Croatian Foreign Ministry

Scope of the statement This was the official justification for reducing the embassy staff. It has not been officially confirmed that the measure directly caused the Rossotrudnichestvo representation to cease operating.

Assessment

Croatia is fundamentally different from Romania. Romania closed a formally established centre, expressly suspended its activity and publicly justified the step by citing propaganda, disinformation and the justification of Russian war crimes.

Croatia, by contrast, negotiated for years over a formal centre, apparently never ratified the special agreement required for it and nevertheless allowed a Rossotrudnichestvo representation to operate. That representation later called itself “Russian House Zagreb”, without any documented stand-alone building or special bilateral status.

1

Centre planned

Russia pursued the project from at least 2004 until 2020.

2

Special agreement not implemented

The planned ratification did not take place, and a formal centre appears never to have been created.

3

Representation used as a brand

Rossotrudnichestvo nevertheless operated in Croatia and later publicly used the “Russian House” name.

That is precisely why the structure could fade almost silently from public view after 2022. There appears to have been no independent institution whose closure required a visible Croatian legal act. The embassy-linked representation likely lost its operational basis, while Croatia had never completed the formal centre project in the first place.

The case demonstrates why this dossier must keep its categories separate: an official Russian Centre, a Rossotrudnichestvo representation, an embassy project, a digital brand and a local association with “Russian House” in its name are not legally or politically interchangeable.

The Russian House in Zagreb was more brand and diplomatic outpost than house. Russia spent at least sixteen years trying to establish a formal centre, but Croatia never ratified the necessary special agreement. After 2022, the substitute structure disappeared from Rossotrudnichestvo’s publicly visible network without any identifiable Croatian closure order.
