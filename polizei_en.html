Conduct of Authorities

Police & Protest

Deployments against a peaceful protest are prompt and resource-intensive, while protection for the demonstrator has repeatedly failed to materialise. The imbalance is documented, and it is now being tested in court.

Detention whose unlawfulness the Berlin Police have conceded
Figures and sequences of events on this page are based on reporting by t-online (May 2025) and Süddeutsche Zeitung (December 2025). The Berlin Police decline to comment on the number and scope of the deployments, citing data protection and ongoing investigations. Statements by those involved are identified as such. Quotations were given in German and are rendered here in English translation; the German wording appears on the German version of this page.
The imbalance, documented
  • At least 33 police deployments took place at the Russian House in 2024 because Lindemeier was standing there with his speaker. This is the figure established by t-online. The Berlin Police themselves decline to state the number of deployments, citing data protection and ongoing investigations. According to t-online's account, the Russian House regularly calls the police on his behalf. t-online, May 2025
  • More than 100 criminal complaints were filed from within the Russian House against Henry Lindemeier, along with three penalty orders (Strafbefehle) for persistent disturbance of the peace and insult. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • After a complaint alleging incitement to hatred, the State Office of Criminal Investigation had the lyrics he had played translated. The finding: the text did not constitute incitement. The investigation nonetheless continued for the time being. t-online, May 2025
  • By May 2025, according to the reporting, none of the allegations of insult had been substantiated; t-online writes of complaints alleging insults that were not insults. On the noise allegations, Lindemeier's lawyer Heinemann says the discussion always starts from zero, because the police name no noise threshold and do not measure the sound. t-online, May 2025
“When a noise complaint is reported, sometimes three patrol cars turn up at the Russian House. When my client was threatened, nobody was available.”
Attorney Christian Wolf · t-online, May 2025 · translated from German
The unlawful detention, 18 December 2024
  • Henry Lindemeier was detained and handcuffed outside the Russian House and taken to a holding cell.
  • Four hours of deprivation of liberty followed, along with identification procedures.
  • The stated grounds were insults and disturbances of the peace in the preceding weeks. Neither was substantiated.
  • In the course of a continuing declaratory action (Fortsetzungsfeststellungsklage), the Berlin Police formally conceded: the operation was unlawful. t-online, May 2025
  • The day before, Lindemeier had called the police himself, after a visitor to the house threatened him that he would one day get a knife in the back. He waited three quarters of an hour. The police did not come. t-online, May 2025
“It is strange that the police keep chasing nonsense.”
Attorney Patrick Heinemann · t-online, May 2025 · translated from German
The BSW incident, May 2025
  • By Lindemeier's account, burly men accompanying BSW party leader Amira Mohamed Ali sought to physically push him aside at the Soviet war memorial in the Tiergarten. t-online, May 2025
  • When he called loudly for the police, the officers told him to leave the site. He was subsequently not permitted to stand in the area. t-online, May 2025
“The group perceived this gentleman's appearance as a disruption of the silent commemoration.”
Police spokesperson to t-online · t-online, May 2025 · translated from German

The police balance competing interests, the spokesperson added.

9 December 2025: A counter-event displaces the protest
  • For that day, the operators of the Russian House registered an event of their own, titled “Don't let yourself be provoked!” Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • Lindemeier learned of it the night before, in an email from the Berlin Police informing him that his demonstration outside the house could not take place that day. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • About twenty officers stood outside the entrance. They were there because of Lindemeier. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • He took up position anyway and let the air-raid siren play. The police issued a dispersal order and escorted him to the other side of the street. Outside the house, his protest did not take place. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • A conversation between Lindemeier and supporters was subsequently classified by the police as an unregistered assembly. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • The SZ writes that Lindemeier sometimes asks himself whose freedom the police are actually defending here. This is the editors' formulation, not a quotation, and therefore appears without quotation marks.
“The Russians have won.”
Henry Lindemeier at the end of the day · Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025 · translated from German
Formally conceded in court proceedings

The detention of 18 December 2024 was unlawful

In the course of a continuing declaratory action, the Berlin Police admitted that the detention and deprivation of liberty took place without legal basis. To date this is the only official acknowledgement of an error in this matter. Whether the sequence of documented incidents shows an isolated failure or a pattern is a question it raises but does not answer.
