Conduct of Authorities

Police & Protest

Deployments against a peaceful protest have been swift and resource-intensive, while protection for the protester has repeatedly failed to materialise. The imbalance is documented, and it is now the subject of legal proceedings.

All quotations on this page are translations from the German original reporting and are marked as such. Where the exact wording could not be verified against the source, the statement is given in reported speech rather than in quotation marks.
The documented imbalance
  • At least 33 police deployments took place at the Russian House during 2024 in connection with the protest. They were prompted by reports from inside the House. t-online, May 2025
  • The management of the Russian House has filed more than 100 complaints against Henry Lindemeier. Three penalty orders were issued, and are being contested. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • In one complaint alleging incitement, the state criminal police had the Russian song lyrics translated and found that they did not constitute incitement. The investigation nonetheless remained open. t-online, May 2025
  • As of May 2025, according to the reporting, none of the allegations of insult or threat raised by the management had been substantiated. In the noise complaints, no sound measurements were carried out and no limit value was named. t-online, May 2025
“When a noise nuisance is reported, sometimes three patrol cars turn up at the Russian House. When my client was threatened, nobody was available.”
Attorney Christian Wolf · translated from t-online, May 2025
The unlawful arrest, 18 December 2024
  • Henry Lindemeier was arrested and handcuffed outside the Russian House and taken to a police facility.
  • He was held for four hours and subjected to forensic identification procedures.
  • The allegations were insult and noise nuisance in the preceding weeks. Neither was substantiated.
  • In proceedings to establish the unlawfulness of the measure, the Berlin police formally conceded that the deployment and the detention had been unlawful. t-online, May 2025
  • On the previous day Lindemeier had called the police himself, after a visitor to the House told him he would get a knife in the back one day. No patrol car was dispatched. t-online, May 2025
“It’s odd that the police keep chasing nonsense.”
Attorney Patrick Heinemann · translated from t-online, May 2025
The BSW incident, May 2025
  • According to t-online, several men from a BSW delegation physically pushed Lindemeier aside.
  • When he called on the police nearby to intervene, the officers instead directed him to leave, and took no action against the men. t-online, May 2025
  • A police spokesperson explained the decision by saying the group had experienced his presence as a disruption of their commemoration. The exact wording of this statement has not been verified against the German original and is therefore given in reported speech.
9 December 2025: a counter-event displaces the protest
  • For this date an event of their own was registered by the operators of the Russian House, directly outside the building. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • Lindemeier learned of it the night before. The police informed him that his assembly could not take place at his established location. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • Around twenty officers secured the entrance. His protest did not take place. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • A conversation between Lindemeier and his supporters was subsequently classified by the police as an unregistered assembly. Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
  • The SZ reports that Lindemeier wonders at times whose freedom the police are actually defending here. This is the newspaper’s narration, not a quotation from him, and is therefore not set in quotation marks.
“The Russians have won.”
Henry Lindemeier at the end of the day · translated from Süddeutsche Zeitung, December 2025
Formally conceded in legal proceedings

The arrest of 18 December 2024 was unlawful

In proceedings to establish the unlawfulness of the measure, the Berlin police conceded that the arrest and detention of Henry Lindemeier had no legal basis. This remains the only official acknowledgement of error in the entire affair. Whether it indicates an isolated failure or a pattern is a question the documented sequence of incidents raises but does not answer.
