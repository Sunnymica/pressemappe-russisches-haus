Belgium country file

Diplomats expelled, the house stayed open

In 2022 Belgium took one of the most far-reaching personnel measures in the EU, expelling 21 Russian embassy and consular staff. The Russian House in Brussels continued to operate and was still inviting audiences in April 2026. The case shows that expelling diplomats and running a cultural centre are two separate levers – and that the Belgian legal basis of the house remains undocumented in public sources.

Expulsion is not enforcement against a house

Belgium expelled 21 Russian staff and implements the EU sanctions. Both are documented – but neither measure is attributed to the Russian House or its employees. The Belgian case shows operational continuity of the house despite far-reaching personnel measures against diplomats. Reading rule: “Open” means the statement is not documented in the public sources examined. It is not proof of the contrary. EU listing, national enforcement, political non-cooperation, treaty termination and actual closure of operations are assessed separately.

Legal basis

For Belgium, no centre-specific founding or status treaty was found. The Belgian-Russian treaty of understanding and cooperation of 8 December 1993 documents the existence of bilateral treaty relations, not any special legal standing of the house.

https://www.senaat.be

The attribution to the Russian embassy rests on the house’s own presentation and on statements by Rossotrudnichestvo. Operator sources document self-presentation and activity, but neither legal personality nor diplomatic status, ownership or host-state authorisation.

https://rushouse.be
https://belgium.rs.gov.ru

Whether the house is notified as part of the mission in the Belgian diplomatic list could not be established from the sources examined. The Belgian protocol guide governs notification and registration of mission staff in general terms but says nothing about this institution.

https://diplomatie.belgium.be

Measures since 2022

On 29 March 2022 Belgium expelled 21 Russian embassy and consular staff, citing espionage and influence operations. The ministry’s annual report examined here documents the diplomatic context but does not itself state the figure of 21.

https://www.reuters.com
https://diplomatie.belgium.be

The Belgian Federal Public Service Finance maintains a continuously updated overview of EU sanctions implementation. It documents general enforcement. No published enforcement act relating to the house – freezing of accounts, a property measure or an operating ban – was found, and none is inferred here from aggregate sanctions figures.

https://finance.belgium.be

Assessment

Operational continuity despite severe personnel measures

Belgium is among the EU states with the most far-reaching personnel measures: 21 expulsions in a single day. Even so, the Russian House in Brussels remained open and was still holding events on site in spring 2026.

For the German comparison, the main lesson is one of terminology: expelling suspected intelligence officers and operating a cultural mission are legally distinct levers. Neither substitutes for the other.

At the same time Belgium illustrates a thin public evidence base. Anyone considering measures against a house must first establish accreditation, legal entity, ownership and the concrete economic resources of the listed agency. In the Belgian case, none of these are publicly available.
