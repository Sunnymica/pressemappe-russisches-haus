Parcel Collection Point at the Russian House

RusPost GmbH – which claims to be the only official branch of the Russian state postal service outside Russia – has been operating a parcel collection point inside the EU-sanctioned Russian House Berlin since 2018. It remains open on weekdays while public prosecutors investigate.

📦
Confirmed – March 2026 The parcel collection service for shipments to Russia continues to operate: Monday–Friday, 12:00–17:30. The operator of the Russian House, Rossotrudnichestvo, is on the EU sanctions list. Public prosecutors have been investigating since April 2024 – targeting unknown individuals and tenants of the Russian House. An initial investigation launched in January 2023 was dropped because the operators hold diplomatic status.

📬 Verified Facts +
  • In May 2018, a branch of the Russian state postal service was opened in the former porter's lodge of the Russian House.Ost-Impuls, May 2018
  • RusPost GmbH is a wholly owned subsidiary of the Russian state postal service. It claims to be the only official branch outside the Russian Federation, though Russian Post itself also references transit exchange offices in other European countries.RusPost GmbH, official website
  • RusPost GmbH is listed as commercially active in the company register. Note that two separate legal entities must be distinguished: RusPost GmbH and the branch office of the joint-stock company “Pochta Rossii”.Berlin Company Register
  • According to on-site observations in March 2026, postal operations at the Russian House continue: Monday–Friday, 12:00–17:30 (break from 15:00 to 16:00).
  • Activist Henry Lindemeier has documented over an extended period that delivery vans regularly load large quantities of parcels directly in front of the Russian House.
  • Individuals entering the Russian House with packages have been clearly documented from the street.
⚠️ Sanctions Law Relevance +
  • Rossotrudnichestvo – the operator of the Russian House – has been on the EU sanctions list since July 2022, requiring all assets to be frozen and prohibiting the provision of any funds or economic resources.
  • This legal restriction mandates no commercial activities, no revenues, and no financial transactions.
  • A parcel collection service is inherently a commercial activity that generates revenue or, at a minimum, facilitates financial flows.
  • Berlin public prosecutors have been investigating since April 2024, targeting “unknown individuals and tenants of the Russian House” for suspected violations of the Foreign Trade and Payments Act.Tagesspiegel / Berliner Zeitung, April 2024
  • An initial investigation launched in January 2023 was dropped because the operators hold diplomatic status, forcing authorities to pursue other legal strategies.Tagesspiegel, September 2023
  • The Central Sanctions Enforcement Office at German Customs classifies the Russian House as a “non-independent branch” of Rossotrudnichestvo, making it directly subject to the sanctions regime.Berliner Zeitung, April 2024
  • While it is not public knowledge whether the postal operations are a specific subject of this investigation, the connection remains highly evident.
  • The Russian state postal service is directly controlled by the Russian government and is not a private enterprise.
🚨 Seized Goods & Criminal Proceedings +
  • Customs authorities discovered prohibited and sanctioned goods in dozens of parcels transported by RusPost from Germany to Moscow, including electronic devices, cash, and jewelry.Bild / FOCUS online, January 27, 2026
  • According to reports, the Berlin Public Prosecutor's Office intends to file formal charges against a senior RusPost manager in connection with these seizures.FOCUS online, January 27, 2026
  • A parallel circumvention network based in Cologne has been identified: a company founded by former employees of a German RusPost subsidiary has been offering shipping services through various Russian supermarkets.
  • Parcels in this network were processed using shipping labels from the Uzbek state postal service (UzPost) – a company that lacks authorization to operate in Germany, according to the Federal Network Agency.FOCUS online, January 27, 2026
  • Journalists sent test packages containing sanctioned items; GPS trackers confirmed that the shipments successfully reached Russia.Bild / FOCUS online, January 27, 2026
  • Insiders report a confidential cooperation agreement between the Russian and Uzbek state postal services, a partnership subsequently confirmed by both entities.FOCUS online, January 27, 2026
🏛️ Origin of Authorization +
  • The branch was established in 2018 – four years after the annexation of Crimea, but four years prior to the full-scale invasion of Ukraine.
  • Which German authorities granted the original authorization – or whether formal approval was even requested – remains unconfirmed to the public.
  • The Federal Foreign Office has declined to comment on the matter.
  • Following the 2022 invasion and the introduction of EU sanctions, this authorization should have been legally reassessed and revoked.
  • Instead, the branch continues its daily operations unimpeded, as though no sanctions were in place.

Open Questions for Politicians and Authorities

  • Was this authorization legally reviewed following the implementation of EU sanctions in 2022?
  • Are customs authorities and public prosecutors actively monitoring the ongoing postal operations at this location?
  • Does this operation violate the Foreign Trade and Payments Act or current EU sanctions regulations?
  • Who monitors the contents of the parcels dispatched to Russia, as well as those arriving from Russia?
  • Is there an administrative or financial link between the Berlin branch and the Cologne circumvention network utilizing Uzbek postal labels?
