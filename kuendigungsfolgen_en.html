Legal situation

What termination would actually mean

It is argued against terminating the 2011 Activities Agreement that Germany would thereby destroy the legal basis for the Goethe-Institut in Russia itself. A look at the 1992 Cultural Agreement shows that this is not accurate in that form.

The Activities Agreement of 4 February 2011 applies to both sides. Article 1 paragraph 1 names them explicitly: the Russian House of Science and Culture in Berlin and the Goethe-Instituts in Moscow, St. Petersburg and Novosibirsk. If it is terminated under Article 12, it ceases to apply to both sides equally.

From this it is concluded that termination would amount to self-harm: Germany would liquidate the legal framework of its own cultural institute. This conclusion presupposes that the 2011 agreement is the only basis in international law for German cultural institutions in Russia. That is not the case.

What the 2011 agreement grants

The treaty text describes a package of special, reciprocally granted rights.

Article 3 grants the centres the rights of a legal person in the respective receiving state. Article 5 paragraph 1 guarantees the public free access to the premises. Article 5 paragraph 2 sets out in a detailed catalogue what the centres may do: language courses and further training programmes for language teachers, conferences, symposia and seminars, exhibitions, film and audiovisual screenings, theatre performances and concerts, libraries with reading rooms, publications, databases and their own internet presence. Article 5 paragraph 3 permits events outside their own premises as well.

Article 8 paragraph 2 provides that seconded staff are accredited as employees of the centres through the diplomatic or consular missions of the receiving state. Paragraph 3 obliges both sides to issue residence permits for seconded staff and their family members as promptly as possible. Paragraph 4 opens the possibility of granting directors of the centres and their deputies diplomatic or consular status upon corresponding notification.

Interim conclusion

The 2011 agreement creates a specific, reciprocal regime governing activities and status. A general basis in international law for cultural institutions already exists alongside it, in the 1992 Cultural Agreement.

The independent basis of 1992

The Agreement on Cultural Cooperation of 16 December 1992 contains its own provision on cultural institutions. Article 14 paragraph 1 reads, in translation:

“The Contracting Parties shall, within the framework of the domestic law applicable in their countries and on terms to be agreed between them, promote the establishment of cultural institutions of the other Contracting Party in the territory of their countries and facilitate their activities.” Agreement on Cultural Cooperation of 16 December 1992, Article 14 paragraph 1 (translation; the German text published in the Federal Law Gazette is authoritative)

Paragraph 2 specifies what this covers, naming explicitly cultural institutes, cultural centres, institutions and representations of scientific organisations, research institutions, universities, schools, teacher training institutions as well as libraries and reading rooms. Paragraph 3 governs the status of these institutions and their seconded specialists through an annex which, according to the treaty text, forms an integral part of the agreement.

The Cultural Agreement entered into force on 18 May 1993. Under Article 18 it runs for five years and is tacitly extended by a further five years in each case unless it is terminated in writing at least six months before the expiry of the respective period of validity. The current period ends on 17 May 2028; notice of termination would have to be given by 17 November 2027 at the latest. It is therefore a separate treaty with its own deadline. Termination of the 2011 Activities Agreement does not affect it.

What would actually cease to apply

Upon termination of the Activities Agreement, the Goethe-Instituts in Russia would lose their special status: the contractually guaranteed rights of activity, accreditation through the diplomatic missions, the entitlement to prompt residence permits and the possibility of diplomatic status for the leadership. The same would apply, in mirror image, to the Russian House in Berlin.

The general basis in international law for cultural institutions of the other side would not cease to apply. Article 14 of the 1992 Cultural Agreement would remain in force. What specific activity would be possible on that basis and under the respective domestic law is a separate question.

Whether an institution could continue to operate in Russia in practice without the special provisions of 2011 is a political question, not a question of treaty law. The answer is unlikely to come as a surprise. It would then, however, lie with the Russian side — and not with a German termination. The difference is not a small one: in the one case Germany itself removes a basis; in the other, it becomes visible under what conditions German cultural work in Russia currently still takes place.

The current situation

The actual scope of activity is part of this assessment. In May 2023 the Russian side capped the staffing levels of German missions and intermediary organisations at 350 persons. According to a report in the Moskauer Deutsche Zeitung of June 2023, the number of Goethe-Institut staff was consequently reduced from around 200 to 13. A few weeks before that decision, the spokeswoman of the Russian Foreign Ministry, Maria Zakharova, had announced “proportionate measures” against the Goethe-Institut in response to pressure from German authorities and media on the Russian House.

As of 2026, the Goethe-Institut website again lists locations in Moscow, St. Petersburg and Novosibirsk offering library and educational services. Operations remain severely restricted compared with the pre-war period; state cooperation is frozen and public events have largely been discontinued.

What is notable is that this presence continues despite the severe Russian restrictions. Which of the special provisions of the 2011 Activities Agreement are actually still being invoked for this residual operation is not publicly apparent.

Two provisions rarely cited

Article 4 paragraph 3 of the Activities Agreement states explicitly that the premises of the centres enjoy neither diplomatic immunity nor inviolability. It does not follow from this that authorities would have access without a legal basis; the general provisions of German law apply to any measures. However, no special inviolability under international law can be derived from the Activities Agreement.

Article 5 paragraph 4 obliges the centres to provide regular information in the host country about planned events. In response to a written question from Member of the Bundestag Robin Wagener, the Federal Foreign Office stated on 21 July 2026 that regular information from the information centres about planned events in the host country had not taken place since 24 February 2022.

Open questions to the Federal Government

Does the Federal Government share the view that Article 14 of the 1992 Cultural Agreement contains a basis for cultural institutions independent of the Activities Agreement? What specific legal consequences would termination of the Activities Agreement have, in its view, for the Goethe-Instituts in Russia — and what consequences for the Russian House in Berlin? What is the current scope of the Goethe-Instituts' activity in Russia, measured by locations, staff, events and participant numbers? And what conclusion does it draw from the fact that a treaty obligation under Article 5 paragraph 4 has not been fulfilled since 24 February 2022?
