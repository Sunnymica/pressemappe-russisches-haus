Russian Houses in Europe: Which ones have already closed?

Several European nations closed their Rossotrudnichestvo centres following the 2022 EU sanctions—including the Republic of Moldova, which is not an EU member. Germany, meanwhile, remains hesitant.

The strongest case: Republic of Moldova

The Republic of Moldova is not an EU member state. It shares a direct border with Ukraine, has Russian troops permanently stationed on its territory in Transnistria, and is economically far more dependent on Moscow than Germany. Yet, Chișinău decided to shut down its Russian House.

Moldovan Culture Minister Cristian Jardan justified the closure in November 2025, stating that the centre was funded by Rossotrudnichestvo—described as “the main state agency exerting the Kremlin’s soft power”—and was actively being used to propagate “distorted Russian narratives.”

When a nation under direct, existential Russian pressure achieves what Germany hesitates to do, it is no longer a matter of feasibility. It is a political choice.

🇱🇹
Lithuania
Closed
Lithuania was one of the first EU countries to act decisively after the 2022 invasion of Ukraine, shutting down the Rossotrudnichestvo centre in Vilnius. Additionally, Vilnius expelled the Russian ambassador and downgraded diplomatic relations to an absolute minimum. TAZ, April 2025 / Lithuania–Russia relations, Wikipedia
🇵🇱
Poland
Closed
Poland closed the Russian cultural centre in Warsaw. As one of Ukraine’s most committed allies, Warsaw has consistently treated Russian state cultural institutions as direct instruments of state propaganda. TAZ, April 2025
🇸🇪
Sweden
Closed
Sweden closed the Russian cultural centre in Stockholm. Following its accession to NATO, Stockholm fundamentally reassessed its national security approach toward Russian state-run institutions. TAZ, April 2025
🇲🇩
Republic of Moldova
Closed — November 2025
The Moldovan government decided in November 2025 to dissolve the Russian House in Chișinău. Culture Minister Cristian Jardan stated that the centre, operated by the sanctioned agency Rossotrudnichestvo, served to spread “distorted Russian narratives.” Despite not being an EU member, Moldova took decisive action. Pravda Deutschland, November 2025
🇨🇿
Czech Republic
Open — Under review
While the Russian House in Prague has not been fully closed, it now operates only a few days per month. Czech authorities, including intelligence services, are reviewing whether to add the centre to the national sanctions list. The institution has been criticized for distributing translated anti-Ukrainian materials. In March 2026, the building was targeted in a Molotov cocktail attack; a suspect subsequently surrendered to police and confessed. EURACTIV, July 2023 / Czech Police, March 2026
🇩🇪
Germany
Open — Despite sanctions
The Russian House in Berlin remains the only major Rossotrudnichestvo branch in the EU operating at full capacity. It continues to run daily, offering a cinema, language courses, a restaurant, and a parcel collection service. The German Federal Government cites the bilateral agreement of 2011 and expresses concerns over the status of the Goethe-Instituts in Russia. However, the current operational agreement can be terminated as of 7 December 2026. SZ, December 2025 / Federal Foreign Office, May 2024

What this means

During the Federal Press Conference on 8 May 2024, the German Federal Foreign Office acknowledged that several EU member states had closed Russian cultural institutions on their soil, justifying Germany’s inaction by pointing to the bilateral agreement and the protection of the Goethe-Instituts. Yet nations like Lithuania, Poland, and Sweden did not allow such agreements to serve as a diplomatic shield. Meanwhile, the Republic of Moldova—confronted with Russian troops on its own territory and operating outside the EU—chose to act anyway.
