Europe dossier · Netherlands country file

No Russian House — but a network without a sign on the door

The Netherlands once had an official Rossotrudnichestvo representation, but apparently never a public cultural centre of its own. By 2017, the national representation had disappeared. Its functions did not vanish: they were distributed across the Russian Embassy, the compatriots council, Russian-language schools, cultural associations, university structures and the Russian House in Brussels.

Status
Former representation disappeared Partner network active No official Russian House established
Core finding

The Netherlands did not close a Russian House after 2022, because the official Rossotrudnichestvo representation had already disappeared by 2017. Its functions, however, survived in a decentralised network of Russian state actors and local partner organisations.

What the official map shows

The Netherlands does not appear as a separate location on Rossotrudnichestvo’s current map or location page. That is accurate, but it must not be confused with the absence of Russian influence structures: the former national representation was replaced by a cross-border and local partner network. Timeline

29 June 2010

Russkiy Mir enters the University of Groningen

The University of Groningen opens a Centre for Russian Studies in its university library. The centre is created explicitly at the initiative of the state-funded Russkiy Mir Foundation. It is intended to serve as a research library, event venue and hub for Russian-Dutch relations. Source: University of Groningen

This was not a Rossotrudnichestvo House, but it performed some of the same outward-facing cultural and academic functions: language, history, events, library services and institutional networking.

August 2013

Official Rossotrudnichestvo representation clearly documented

Rossotrudnichestvo head Konstantin Kosachev meets the Dutch compatriots council at the Russian Trade Representation in Amsterdam. Zurab Agirbov is expressly identified as Rossotrudnichestvo’s representative in the Netherlands. Source: Pravfond

The venue is telling: not a dedicated cultural centre, but the Russian Trade Representation. This suggests that the presence was administratively attached to existing Russian state structures abroad.

July 2014

Events delivered through partners rather than a dedicated house

The Dutch Rossotrudnichestvo representation and the Russian Embassy jointly organise a Roerich exhibition at the Russian honorary consulate in Maastricht, with support from a cultural association in Eindhoven. The exhibition later travels to the Russian Centre for Science and Culture in Brussels. Source: International Centre of the Roerichs

Rossotrudnichestvo coordinated the project but apparently provided no venue of its own in the Netherlands.

10 August 2014

Local association “Russisch Huis” cooperates with Rossotrudnichestvo

A “Russian Lunch” is held at the Russian honorary consulate in Maastricht. The Russian-speaking community’s own account names the local association “Russisch Huis” as organiser and explicitly states that it worked in cooperation with Rossotrudnichestvo. The initiator also refers to active Russian communities in Amsterdam, Rotterdam and The Hague. Source: Russian Community Maastricht

Not the same as the national representation The evidence shows a local association cooperating with Rossotrudnichestvo. It does not prove that the association formed part of the state representation, received state funding or operated under state instructions.
July/August 2014

Hotline after the downing of flight MH17

Following the downing of flight MH17, the Rossotrudnichestvo representation and Russian consular structures establish a hotline for Russian citizens and so-called compatriots. Agirbov continues to appear as the agency’s local representative. Source: World Coordinating Council of Russian Compatriots

November 2017

The national representation has disappeared

At the Dutch compatriots conference, the chair of the coordinating council states that Rossotrudnichestvo is no longer represented in the Netherlands. She expressly asks the embassy and Moscow authorities to help restore a representative. Source: Rossaprimavera

The time frame can therefore be narrowed down: the representation was active in 2014 and no longer existed by late 2017. There is no evidence of a Dutch closure decision or withdrawal of accreditation.

2019

Moscow coordination without a Dutch national representative

The compatriots conference includes representatives of the Russian Embassy, the Russian Foreign Ministry and a Rossotrudnichestvo official responsible for compatriots abroad. No dedicated national representative for the Netherlands is named. The agenda covers Russian schools, major events, historical remembrance and consolidation of the diaspora. Source: Diplomat Magazine

September 2021

The Russian House in Brussels takes over the link

The Dutch compatriots conference brings together embassy staff, Russian-language schools, cultural associations, media actors, clergy and youth organisations. Vera Bunina, head of the Russian House in Brussels, addresses the conference by video, while messages from the head of Rossotrudnichestvo and the Russian Foreign Ministry are read out. Source: KCOPC

This shows a cross-border division of responsibility: the Brussels House partly replaced the missing Dutch House as a Rossotrudnichestvo point of contact.

1 March 2022

University of Groningen freezes non-university Russian ties

The University of Groningen freezes all relations with Russian partners that are not universities, with immediate effect. This likely covered its institutional connection with the Russkiy Mir Foundation. The university’s own Centre for Russian Studies is not dissolved. Source: University of Groningen

The centre remains listed as a university expertise centre in 2026. It is unclear which collections, licences, legacy agreements or other links from the Russkiy Mir founding period remain in place. Source: University of Groningen

March 2022

Seventeen Russian intelligence officers expelled

The Netherlands expels 17 Russian intelligence officers operating under diplomatic cover. The government describes them as a direct threat to Dutch national security. Source: Dutch government

The measure did not specifically target Rossotrudnichestvo, but substantially reduced the staffing and operating space of Russian state structures in the country.

21 July 2022

EU sanctions Rossotrudnichestvo and Russkiy Mir

The European Union lists both organisations under its sanctions regime. Rossotrudnichestvo is described as the main state agency projecting Kremlin soft power and hybrid influence. Source: EUR-Lex

Dutch authorities, companies, banks and associations may therefore no longer make funds or economic resources available to either organisation, directly or indirectly.

18 February 2023

Diplomatic cap and closure of the Trade Representation

After Russia continues trying to post intelligence officers under diplomatic cover, the Netherlands caps the number of bilateral Russian diplomats at the level of Dutch diplomatic staffing in Russia. Source: government letter

The government also orders the Russian Trade Representation in Amsterdam to close — the same organisational environment in which the Rossotrudnichestvo representative had appeared in 2013. The building remains Russian state property and retains protection under international law, but may no longer operate as a trade mission.

2022–2026

The compatriots network remains active

KCOPC/KSORS continues publishing events, competitions, offers from Russian-language schools, commemorations and calls for participation in Russian and international projects. Documented activities in 2026 include an “Immortal March” in Amsterdam, concerts and children’s competitions. Source: KCOPC

Since June 2022, the platform has also maintained a “Legal Support” section. Its funding and institutional sponsorship remain unclear.

Legal basis

1. Diplomatic and consular structures

Where a Rossotrudnichestvo representation operates within the organisational environment of the Russian Embassy and its staff hold diplomatic status, the Vienna Convention and Dutch accreditation decisions apply. Source: Vienna Convention

Article 9 permits a receiving state to declare a diplomat persona non grata, Article 11 allows it to limit the size of a mission, and Article 12 requires consent for additional offices outside the mission’s seat.

A stand-alone Russian House could not therefore acquire diplomatic status merely because Russia called it one. Dutch consent, legal form and any applicable bilateral agreement would be decisive.

2. EU sanctions against Rossotrudnichestvo and Russkiy Mir

Both organisations have been subject to asset freezes since 21 July 2022. It is prohibited to make funds or economic resources available to them, directly or indirectly. Source: EUR-Lex

This can include payments, premises, services or projects channelled through intermediary associations where they ultimately benefit a listed organisation.

3. Dutch implementation

EU sanctions regulations apply directly in the Netherlands. Breaches are prosecuted under the Sanctions Act 1977 and related sanctions orders. No separate Dutch ban on a non-existent “Russian House” was required in order to cut off financial relations with Rossotrudnichestvo. Source: Dutch government

A new law on implementing international sanctions was introduced in parliament in February 2026 but remains under legislative consideration. Source: bill

4. Legal status of KCOPC/KSORS

The coordinating council describes itself as a “Vereniging met beperkte rechtsbevoegdheid”, an association with limited legal capacity under Dutch law. Formally, it is not a Russian state authority and cannot automatically be equated with Rossotrudnichestvo. Source: KCOPC statutes

Its own statutes nevertheless provide for cooperation with the Russian Embassy, regional and global compatriots councils and other diaspora organisations. Its stated aims include ties with the “historical homeland”, promotion of a positive image of Russia, Russian-language media work, youth work and implementation of decisions taken at international compatriots conferences.

Legally, KCOPC remains a Dutch association. Functionally, it is an embedded node in the official Russian compatriots architecture.

Measures since 2022

The Netherlands did not close a Russian House. Instead, it targeted the state and institutional structures through which Russian presence was organised:

  • expulsion of 17 Russian intelligence officers operating under diplomatic cover
  • a reciprocal cap on bilateral Russian diplomats
  • closure of the Russian Trade Representation in Amsterdam
  • implementation of EU sanctions against Rossotrudnichestvo and Russkiy Mir
  • freezing of non-university Russian ties by the University of Groningen

Security policy, not cultural prohibition The Dutch government acted against intelligence cover, diplomatic overstaffing, the Trade Representation and sanction-sensitive financial relations. No public decision has been found banning Russian-language cultural activity or KCOPC as such.

Government rationale in the original

There is no centre-specific explanation because no official Dutch Russian House had existed since at least 2017. The relevant official statements concern intelligence activity, diplomatic restrictions and the Trade Representation.

Assessment

The Dutch case is not one in which a Russian House was closed after the full-scale invasion. A small official Rossotrudnichestvo representation existed for a time but had disappeared by late 2017. Cultural, educational and diaspora-related functions were then redistributed across a network.

1

Official representation

Documented in 2013 and 2014; gone by 2017.

2

Partner network

Embassy, KCOPC, schools, cultural associations, honorary consulates and university partners.

3

Cross-border support

The Russian House in Brussels partly took over the Rossotrudnichestvo connection.

The absence of an official House must therefore not be confused with the absence of Russian influence structures. The Dutch model was decentralised: a representative coordinated, while local partners supplied venues, audiences and social access. The Maastricht primary source provides a concrete example of a local association named “Russisch Huis” cooperating explicitly with Rossotrudnichestvo. Such local structures remained after the national representative had disappeared.

The compatriots conferences show how the embassy, the Russian Foreign Ministry, Rossotrudnichestvo, the Russian House in Brussels, schools, media, churches, cultural associations and youth groups were brought together within a common framework.

At the same time, careful distinctions remain essential. Not every Russian-language school, cultural association or participant in a KCOPC event is a Kremlin instrument. Even documented cooperation with Rossotrudnichestvo does not by itself prove state ownership, funding or command. Those elements must be established separately through financial flows, contracts and personal connections.

Not to be confused The historic “Russenhuis” at Lange Voorhout 78 in The Hague was a former residence for stateless Russian and Belarusian refugees. It operated from 1956 to 1978 and had no connection with Rossotrudnichestvo or the present-day Russian influence network. Source: Haagse Historie

What is established is that KCOPC openly provides for cooperation with the Russian Embassy and international compatriots structures and presents itself as a representative body in dealings with Russian state institutions.

The Netherlands did not remove a sign from a door. There had long been no such sign. Instead, it targeted the diplomatic, intelligence, trade and financial nodes of the Russian state network, while a decentralised cultural and diaspora infrastructure continued to operate underneath.
