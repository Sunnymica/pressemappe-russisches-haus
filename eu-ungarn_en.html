Hungary country file

Embedded in the embassy – with an unclear treaty chain

The Budapest house is an official institution embedded in the Russian embassy structure, not a mere partner organisation. It remained active on site after February 2022 and after the EU listing. Legally the case is less clear-cut than Greece or Spain: the 1993 agreement recognises the centres but refers their support to a separate intergovernmental agreement whose text was not found.

No measure found – but no clean bill of health either

No measure relating to the house was found for Hungary; no particular current government line on the house is officially verified. The case again shows that an EU listing does not automatically lead to physical closure. It does not show that all transactions of the house remained permissible or unrestricted. Reading rule: “Open” means the statement is not documented in the public sources examined. It is not proof of the contrary. EU listing, national enforcement, political non-cooperation, treaty termination and actual closure of operations are assessed separately.

Legal basis

The Hungarian treaty position consists of several layers. The 1979 agreement, promulgated by Law-Decree 27/1980, expressly names the Soviet house in Budapest in Article 13 – and still stands in the Hungarian legal database.

https://njt.hu

The 1993 agreement, promulgated as Government Decree 148/1998, recognises the centres in Article 9 but refers their support to a separate intergovernmental agreement. Which instrument is meant, and whether it is in force, was not established.

https://njt.hu

It therefore remains open whether the 1979/1980 instrument still applies in parallel or was materially replaced by later arrangements. As to the property, a heritage database describes the building as Russian state property; without a land register extract that remains a secondary statement.

https://njt.hu
https://budapest100.hu

Assessment

Embassy embedding and an unclear treaty chain

Two features are decisive for Hungary: embedding in the embassy structure and the unclear chain of several older treaties. Both complicate assessment – and both are instructive for the German debate.

The case again shows that an EU listing does not automatically lead to physical closure. It does not show that all transactions of the house remained permissible or unrestricted. The absence of published measures is a search finding, not a clean bill of health.

Methodologically Hungary shows how difficult matters become when a treaty refers to a further, untraceable agreement. Without that text, status, privileges and termination options cannot be determined.
