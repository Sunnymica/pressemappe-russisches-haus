Europe dossier · Country file: Bulgaria

Sofia acknowledged the sanctions problem — yet the Russian cultural centre remains active

The Russian Cultural and Information Centre at 34 Shipka Street is an official overseas representation of Rossotrudnichestvo. Bulgaria explicitly acknowledged the centre’s potential sanctions exposure in 2024, but no such measure was implemented. In 2026 the tax authority found taxable economic activity without the corresponding tax returns; the centre nevertheless remained open.

Status
Official site active Tax proceedings pending Sanctions enforcement unresolved
Key finding

Bulgaria is a case of announced but unimplemented measures. The government acknowledged the centre’s potential sanctions exposure in 2024. In 2026 the tax authority found taxable economic activity without the corresponding tax returns and tax payments. The centre remains active.

Timeline

19. April 1993

Agreement on culture, education and science

Bulgaria and Russia conclude the Agreement on Cooperation in Culture, Education and Science. Later intergovernmental cooperation programmes are based on it.

April 2018

Youth festival and flight simulator

The “Fotonika” simulator is presented at the Russian Cultural and Information Centre during a Russian youth festival for scientific and technical creativity. Later listings name the Su-33, Su-25T, MiG-29 and F-16 and include air combat and attacks on ground targets; vouchers are explicitly offered from the age of six.

Juni 2023

Protests after propaganda film “Azovstal”

A screening of the Russian film “Azovstal” prompts protests and a petition calling for the centre’s closure. More than 3,500 signatures are collected in the first four days.

Source: Svobodna Evropa

Februar 2024

Foreign minister supports possible asset freeze

Then Deputy Prime Minister and Foreign Minister Mariya Gabriel tells parliament that the centre’s accounts could be frozen and that her ministry would support such a step. At the same time, she points to unclear domestic responsibilities for sanctions enforcement.

23. April 2024

Investigation into schools and kindergartens

Sega journalist Doroteya Dachkova documents numerous visits by municipal schools and kindergartens after 24 February 2022 following a freedom-of-information request. One school reports six visits involving 449 children from 19 classes, another 134 third-grade pupils, and a third an event attended by 300 pupils.

Source: Sega

März–August 2026

Tax inspection and ordered audit

The National Revenue Agency finds that the centre carries out taxable economic activity but has filed no annual tax returns and paid no corresponding taxes. The inspection ends on 20 March 2026; an audit for 2021–2025 is ordered in April. By early August it has still not begun.

Legal basis

1. The 1993 agreement

Cultural cooperation is based on the agreement of 19 April 1993. Later bilateral cooperation programmes rely on it. Its duration and termination mechanism are still being reviewed for this country file.

2. EU sanctions

Rossotrudnichestvo has been on the EU sanctions list since 21 July 2022. In 2024 the Bulgarian government explicitly acknowledged the centre’s potential sanctions exposure; no corresponding measure has yet been implemented.

3. Tax law

The Bulgarian tax authority treats the centre as a Sofia-based legal entity carrying out taxable economic activity. The audit ordered in 2026 covers the years 2021–2025.

4. Outstanding treaty question

It remains unclear whether a separate agreement specifically governing the centre exists alongside the general 1993 agreement. Ownership of the property at 34 Shipka Street also remains to be verified.

Work with children and young people

The Sega investigation shows that the centre’s contacts with schools and kindergartens were not limited to isolated private visits. Documented activities include class visits, recitation competitions, a historical quiz and other events after the start of Russia’s full-scale invasion.

Velina Todorova, Vice-Chair of the UN Committee on the Rights of the Child, did not regard Russian-language teaching and practical language use as inherently problematic. In view of Russia’s war of aggression, however, participation by children in events organised and financed by the centre should, she said, be carefully reassessed and monitored.

Fighter-aircraft simulator inside the cultural centre

The centre also rents space to third parties. In the basement, a Bulgarian operator runs the “Fotonika” fighter-aircraft simulator. Listings include aerobatics, air combat and attacks on ground targets; one voucher is explicitly valid from the age of six. The device is not owned by Rossotrudnichestvo but is located inside the cultural centre operated by the sanctioned agency.

Assessment

Bulgaria presents a different pattern from Romania or Moldova. The political and sanctions problem was recognised but has so far led neither to closure nor to visible enforcement against the centre. Instead, a tax proceeding developed in 2026 and still had not begun months after it was ordered.

The centre remains active — despite EU sanctions against its operator, despite announced possible asset measures, and despite a tax inspection that found economic activity without the corresponding returns and payments.
