Facts & Legal Background

All verified facts regarding the Russian House Berlin—compiled from national media reports, official documents, and other clearly identified sources. Click a panel to open.

🏛️ The Institution +
  • 29,000 m² of floor space on Friedrichstraße, Berlin-Mitte—making it one of the largest Russian cultural institutes in the world.Süddeutsche Zeitung, Dec. 2025
  • Opened in 1984 in East Berlin, the centre continued operating as a Russian cultural venue following German reunification.TAZ, Apr. 2025
  • Operated by Rossotrudnichestvo, a Russian federal agency subordinate to the Ministry of Foreign Affairs.t-online, May 2025
  • Rossotrudnichestvo was added to the EU sanctions list in July 2022 for spreading Kremlin narratives and actively undermining Ukrainian sovereignty.Tages-Anzeiger, Feb. 2026
  • While director Pavel Izvolskiy claims independence from Rossotrudnichestvo, archived versions of the website confirm the connection; the reference has since been removed.Tages-Anzeiger, Feb. 2026
  • The institution continues operating despite sanctions, offering language courses, concerts, theatre productions, a restaurant, a bookshop, and a cinema.Süddeutsche Zeitung, Dec. 2025
  • The bookshop temporarily sold decorative soap in the shape of hand grenades, tanks, and handguns.TAZ, Apr. 2025
  • The cinema screened propaganda films produced by the banned state broadcaster RT depicting Ukrainians as fascists, as well as a retrospective of director Karen Shakhnazarov—who has publicly justified the war of aggression and threatened Kremlin opponents with “concentration camps, re-education, and sterilization.”Wikipedia / Berliner Kurier / Süddeutsche Zeitung
  • In 2018, the Russian House screened the film “Donbass”—which, according to a study by the Ukrainian Institute in Kyiv, contains complete fabrications regarding alleged civilian executions by the Azov Battalion.Ukrainian Institute Kyiv, 2024
  • Since March 2025, the Russian House has hosted the exhibition “The Russian Soul of the White Rose” under the official patronage of the Russian Embassy. An analysis by Regina Laska indicates the exhibition posthumously instrumentalizes resistance fighter Alexander Schmorell to serve the state narrative that Russia is Europe’s anti-fascist liberator.antwortseiten.org, 19 June 2025 · Regina Laska
  • On 9 April 2026, the Russian House screened the film “The Challenge”—a co-production between Roscosmos and Russian state-run Channel One. Vladimir Putin personally presented the director and leading actress with a State Prize at the Kremlin.Wikipedia / russisches-haus.de, April 2026
💶 Finances & the State +
  • Germany covers €70,000 in annual property taxes for the building out of its federal budget, pursuant to the 2013 bilateral agreement.Süddeutsche Zeitung, Dec. 2025
  • In 2023, the Deutsche Bundesbank refused to release requested frozen funds for operating costs; observers cited by SZ interpreted the volume of the requested sums as evidence of ongoing commercial operations.Süddeutsche Zeitung, Dec. 2025
  • Administrative court ruling, 24 June 2025: The Bundesbank’s decision was legally upheld (the specific court instance and whether the ruling is legally binding are currently being verified).Süddeutsche Zeitung, Dec. 2025
  • How the building’s operational costs have been covered since the ruling remains unanswered due to a lack of response to media inquiries.Süddeutsche Zeitung, Dec. 2025
  • The doorbells at the premises show numbers only, with no names, leaving it unclear whether and to whom the residential apartments in the building are being leased.Süddeutsche Zeitung / Tages-Anzeiger, 2025–2026

  • The Berlin Commissioner for Data Protection is investigating the scope and legality of the building's external camera surveillance; the review remains pending.Tages-Anzeiger, Feb. 2026

📄 German-Russian Agreement 2011 +
Signed 4 February 2011, in force since 7 June 2012

Agreement on the Activities of Cultural and Information Centres

This bilateral treaty governs the activities of the Russian House in Berlin and the Goethe-Instituts in the Russian Federation. It does not address questions of property or land ownership, which are regulated by a separate agreement (see the 2013 Agreement).

→ The operational agreement is up for renewal every five years, subject to six months' prior notice. The upcoming deadline to submit a notice of termination is 6 December 2026.
📄 German-Russian Agreement 2013 +
Signed 15 May 2013, in force since 5 June 2013

Agreement on the Accomodation of the Russian House (Land & Building)

Grants the Russian Federation the unlimited, rent-free right to use the state-owned property on Friedrichstraße. Furthermore, it obligates the Federal Republic of Germany to pay approximately €70,000 in annual property taxes from the federal budget. The treaty was signed for a term of 99 years, remaining nominally binding until 2112.

→ While the treaty does not contain ordinary termination clauses, legal expert Jens Baganz argues that the doctrine of a fundamental change of circumstances (Art. 62 of the Vienna Convention on the Law of Treaties) provides solid legal grounds for termination or suspension.
🇪🇺 EU Sanctions: Rossotrudnichestvo +
July 2022

EU places Rossotrudnichestvo on restrictive measures list

Legal consequence: All assets of the agency are frozen, and there is a strict prohibition against making funds or economic resources available, directly or indirectly, to Rossotrudnichestvo. Under EU sanctions law, the commercial operations and revenues of the Berlin branch are highly problematic. While the 2011 treaty theoretically allows the centres to recover costs and generate revenue through specific sales, whether this practice violates subsequently enacted EU sanctions remains the core legal controversy.

→ The Russian House continues to charge fees for language courses, run a commercial restaurant, and host ticketed concerts, representing potential ongoing violations of EU sanctions.
⚖️ Court Decision 2025 – Electricity Bill +
24 June 2025

Administrative court ruling on the release of frozen funds

The Russian House initiated legal proceedings against the Deutsche Bundesbank after the latter refused to authorize the release of frozen financial assets intended to settle outstanding electricity bills.

→ According to SZ reports, the administrative court ruled in favor of the Bundesbank’s restrictive stance. The specific court instance and whether the ruling has become final and legally binding are currently being verified. It remains unknown how the institution is currently financing its ongoing operational overhead.
⚖️ Unlawful Arrest of Lindemeier +
18 December 2024

Declaratory relief proceedings against the Berlin Police

Henry Lindemeier was arrested in front of the Russian House, placed in handcuffs, and detained for four hours to record his identity. The initial allegations of criminal insult and breach of the peace were unsubstantiated. During subsequent declaratory court actions, the police department formally conceded that the entire operation and detention had been unlawful.

→ The Berlin Police formally admitted the illegality of the arrest in court, resulting in a successful legal outcome for the plaintiff.
🔍 Ongoing Investigations +
Ongoing since 2024

Berlin Public Prosecutor’s Office — Foreign Trade and Payments Act

A criminal investigation is underway regarding potential violations of German foreign trade laws. Public reports indicate that investigators are also reviewing the legal status of the residential apartments inside the building; currently, the doorbells list only numbers instead of tenant names.

→ The criminal proceedings remain active. An official conclusion is pending.
Ongoing since 2024

Berlin Commissioner for Data Protection — Video Surveillance

An investigation is assessing whether the institution’s external security cameras illegally recorded activities on public streets. Footage from these cameras was notably utilized in police files against protestor Henry Lindemeier.

→ The regulatory investigation is ongoing. No final decision has been announced.
December 2025 / January 2026

European Commission (DG FISMA) — Sanctions Complaint

A formal complaint was lodged with the European Commission concerning Germany’s potential failure to enforce EU sanctions law regarding the continued operations of the Russian House. Registered under reference number CPLT(2026)00148. The Commission is currently reviewing the submission based on European Union law.

→ The complaint has been registered. Should the Commission identify a breach of EU law by Germany and decide to take formal action, it may initiate infringement proceedings.
