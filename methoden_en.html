The Methods of the Russian House

Criminal complaints, physical assaults, and RT propaganda: documented incidents surrounding the protests at the Russian House since 2024 – individually cited and clustered in a conspicuous timeframe. Whether this points to coordinated effort remains an open question.

The Russian House Berlin is operated by Rossotrudnichestvo, which has been on the EU sanctions list since July 2022. According to the German Federal Government, the house is a non-independent department of the sanctioned entity, and its assets are frozen. Since 2024, individuals protesting against the institution or reporting on it publicly have faced a striking series of retaliatory measures.

This page documents these events individually: over one hundred criminal complaints (none of which have led to charges), physical assaults, and threats outside the venue. Additionally, it highlights coverage by the EU-banned Russian state media outlet RT, which relies heavily on sources close to the Russian House. Each incident is supported by verified sources and dates. While coordination has not been legally proven, the intense temporal clustering of these events is a documented fact.

⚖️

Legal Intimidation

Over 100 criminal complaints filed against Henry Lindemeier. One of these complaints names journalist Regina Laska as an accomplice – a charge she denies, as she was abroad at the time. Listed as a witness is attorney Thomas Worm, whose registered law office address is identical to that of the Russian House.

📺

RT Propaganda Campaigns

Repeated coverage on the banned Russian state channel RT, which has framed a protester as a threat to children and visually linked him to an act of terrorism through targeted image placement.

🥊

Physical Assaults

A documented physical attack on a demonstrator outside the building, alongside ongoing threats. This includes an unlawful arrest of a protester, the illegality of which was later formally conceded by the police during administrative court proceedings.

    RT Image Manipulation
    Prague Arson Attack: RT Visually Links Lindemeier to Terrorism
    Following an arson attack on the Russian House in Prague, RT publishes an article. The RT attempt to link Lindemeier to the attack was thus not only baseless, but factually disproven.RT DE, March 27, 2026 / Czech Police, March 31, 2026

Attorney Thomas Worm – His Documented Role

  • Worm's law office is listed in official directories at Friedrichstraße 176–179 – the exact address of the Russian House (directory entries date back to 2019; Lindemeier has repeatedly observed him on the premises).
  • He is listed as a witness in the criminal complaint that names journalist Regina Laska as an accomplice. Whether his connection to the building constitutes a professional conflict of interest is currently the subject of an active professional misconduct complaint.
  • He served as the interviewee for the RT article published on December 12, 2025, which characterized Lindemeier's protest as the “harassment of children.”
  • During the counter-protest on December 9, 2025 (mobilized via Telegram by Integral e.V. in scheduled shifts), Lindemeier observed him giving personal instructions to participants, noting that “the security staff at the entrance know him.”
  • A professional misconduct complaint has been filed against Thomas Worm with the Berlin Bar Association (Rechtsanwaltskammer) – the outcome is pending; the filing itself does not establish legal guilt.
  • The recipient of any rent payments for his office space remains publicly unknown. If these funds benefit the sanctioned organization, it would raise significant legal issues under EU sanctions regulations, according to legal experts cited by the NZZ. The public prosecutor's office is investigating subtenants in the building.

RT DE – A Banned Outlet as an Instrument of Propaganda

RT DE (Russia Today Germany) has been banned in the EU since March 2022, and access to its website is restricted in Germany. Nevertheless, RT DE regularly publishes articles focusing on the Russian House Berlin and its critics. This coverage relies heavily on sources close to the center; in the article from December 12, 2025, attorney Thomas Worm served as the primary source.

The pattern: incidents involving the Russian House are portrayed exclusively from the institution's perspective. Critics are framed as provocateurs or threats to children; in Lindemeier's case, he was visually associated with a terrorist act. Counter-perspectives or reports of assaults originating from within the building are entirely omitted.

The visual framing of Lindemeier in connection with the Prague arson attack represents the most severe manifestation of this strategy to date.
